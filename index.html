Opening & printing

How do I open the PDF on my phone?

You don't need the PDF to read: the Guide tab opens every page right here, with zoom. To keep the file, tap PDF › Download.

  • iPhone: it opens in Safari. Tap Share › Save to Files (or open it in Books).
  • Android: it goes to your Downloads folder. Open it with Files or Google Drive.

The file is 31 MB, so use Wi-Fi.

How do I open it on my computer?

Click PDF › Download. It opens in your browser (Chrome, Edge or Safari) or in Adobe Acrobat Reader, which is free. Later, you'll find it in your Downloads folder. You can also log in here on the computer and read it in the app.

How do I print it?
  • Paper: Letter (8.5 × 11 in).
  • Scale: “Fit to page” (or “Fit to printable area”), so nothing gets cut. The pages are A4 size, so you'll see a thin white border.
  • Color or black & white? Color is best: the maps use color to tell the rows apart. Black & white works for the Preparation and the steps; on the maps, follow the row numbers.
  • Save ink: print only the pages you need. Each style is 3 pages in a row (Style 03 = pages 23–25), and the Guide tab shows every page number. Print the Preparation (pages 5–16) once and keep it.

Braiding with the guide

How many packs of hair do I buy?

Page 7 (How Many Packs) has a chart by braid size and length, for adults and kids. Each style's Style Profile (its first page) shows the packs for that style.

  • Always buy 1 extra pack, in the same color number.
  • A 3X pack holds 3 bundles: count it as 3.
  • Thick hair or a bigger head? Add 1 pack.
  • 13 Two-Strand Twists and 14 Mini Braids use your own hair, no extensions.
I'm a beginner. Where do I start?
  • Read page 2 (How to Use This Guide).
  • Read the Preparation once, pages 5–16.
  • Pick a Level 1 style (01–15). 03 Medium Knotless is the classic first style.
  • Braid one row at the nape and check it against the map before doing the whole head.
  • Page 16 lists the 8 mistakes beginners make most, with the fix for each.
I'm left-handed. Does the guide work for me?

Yes. The steps are the same; many left-handed braiders just work from the other side. Page 11 (Grip & Tension) explains overhand and underhand: pick the grip that feels natural and keep it on every braid. That's what keeps them even.

Bonus 1, Do It On Yourself, has a short left-handed checklist on page 8.

My braids come out loose or uneven.
  • Different sizes: part the whole row first and follow the squares on that style's Map (its 2nd page). Page 9 shows how to part.
  • Thick and thin along the braid: keep the same grip from root to end (page 11).
  • Loose or bulky at the root: braid 2–3 stitches of your own hair before the first feed-in, and keep each feed-in piece about ⅓ of your section (page 10).
  • Hands slipping: a little gel on your fingertips. Rest your arms: tired arms braid loose.
  • Stop after the first row and check it against the map (page 16). Fixing 7 braids is easy; fixing 60 isn't.
How long will a style take me?

Every Style Profile shows the hours for a beginner, and every Map splits the head into 3 work sections with the time for each, so you can braid in sessions.

Level 1 goes from 1–2 h (08 Straight-Back Cornrows) to 7–10 h (03 Medium Knotless). Small and micro styles take the longest (32 Micro Braids: 16–22 h). You get faster with practice. In the Guide tab, filter the styles by time.

Can I use it on my daughter?

Yes. Page 13 (Braiding a Child) covers short sessions, a gentle grip and lighter styles. 13 of the 50 styles are shown on a girl: tap Kids in the Guide tab. Start with 08 Straight-Back Cornrows (1–2 h) or 11 Knotless with Beads (3–5 h).

Keep your grip lighter than on an adult; if she says it hurts, loosen that braid. Bonus 2 adds 20 back-to-school styles.

It feels tight or it hurts. What do I do?

Firm at the root, never pulling the skin (page 11). If it hurts or you see bumps at the root, stop and loosen or redo that braid, and use a lighter grip on the edges and the nape. The guide teaches styling technique; it isn't medical advice.
